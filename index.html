How I think

How I make product decisions.

Answers to questions I'm often asked, so you know how I work before we talk.

I start from the business goal and the customer problem, then work out what it costs to build. With 150+ stories in a backlog, everything looks important. So I ask what moves the metric we agreed on, what carries risk if we wait (in banking, that's usually fraud or compliance), and what the team can actually ship well. Then I say no, clearly, to the rest and explain why.

I've written migration scripts and shipped patches through CI/CD, so I can talk trade-offs with engineers in their terms. I write acceptance criteria that testers can use without asking me what I meant. That's a big part of why rework dropped 45% on the ICICI modules I owned.

I treat them as one problem. Smart Lock is my example: customers got a simple way to lock access to their own account, and fraud cases fell 30%. When outages blocked fixed deposits, we built a fallback instead of showing an error page, and 30K+ deposits went through. Trust comes from protection that's easy to use.

Where it removes slow, repetitive work and a person still makes the decision that matters. I built PM Copilot to test this on my own job: it drafts a PRD, market sizing, user stories and risks in under 90 seconds, and I refine them. In fintech, I'd apply the same idea to fraud review, customer service and compliance checks.
